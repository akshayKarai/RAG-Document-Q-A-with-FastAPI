Introduction to Artificial Intelligence
=======================================

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the simulation of human intelligence processes by machines,
especially computer systems. AI research has been defined as the field of study of intelligent
agents, which refers to any system that perceives its environment and takes actions that
maximize its chance of achieving its goals.

History of AI
--------------
The term "Artificial Intelligence" was coined by John McCarthy in 1956 at the Dartmouth
Conference, which is widely considered the founding moment of AI as a discipline. Early
AI research in the 1950s and 1960s explored topics such as problem solving and symbolic
methods. In the 1990s, AI research developed methods for dealing with uncertain or incomplete
information.

Deep Learning Revolution
--------------------------
Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers
(hence "deep") to learn from data. The 2012 ImageNet competition marked a turning point
when AlexNet, a deep neural network created by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and
Geoffrey Hinton, dramatically outperformed all other competitors.

Key AI Applications
--------------------
1. Natural Language Processing (NLP): AI systems that can understand and generate human
   language. Examples include translation, sentiment analysis, and question answering.

2. Computer Vision: AI systems that can interpret and understand visual information from
   the world. Applications include facial recognition, medical imaging, and autonomous vehicles.

3. Robotics: The integration of AI with physical machines to perform tasks in the real world.
   Modern robots can learn from demonstrations and adapt to new environments.

4. Recommendation Systems: AI that analyzes user preferences and behavior to suggest relevant
   content. Used widely by Netflix, YouTube, Amazon, and Spotify.

Large Language Models
----------------------
Large Language Models (LLMs) are neural networks trained on vast amounts of text data.
They can generate coherent text, answer questions, write code, and perform many other
language-based tasks. Notable LLMs include GPT-4 (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic), Gemini
(Google), and LLaMA (Meta).

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
--------------------------------------
RAG is an AI framework that combines information retrieval with text generation. Instead
of relying solely on knowledge embedded in model parameters, RAG systems first retrieve
relevant documents from a knowledge base, then use that context to generate more accurate
and up-to-date answers. RAG reduces hallucinations and allows models to access private
or recent information.

The typical RAG pipeline:
- Document ingestion: Split documents into chunks and embed them into vectors
- Indexing: Store vectors in a vector database (e.g., FAISS, Pinecone, Chroma)
- Retrieval: Embed user query and find top-k similar document chunks
- Generation: Pass retrieved context + query to an LLM to generate the final answer

Ethical Considerations in AI
------------------------------
As AI systems become more capable, several ethical issues have emerged:
- Bias and Fairness: AI models can perpetuate or amplify societal biases present in training data
- Transparency: The "black box" nature of deep learning makes decisions hard to explain
- Privacy: AI systems often require large amounts of personal data to train effectively
- Safety: Ensuring AI systems behave as intended and do not cause unintended harm
- Job Displacement: Automation powered by AI may displace certain categories of human work

Future of AI
-------------
Researchers are actively working on Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) — AI systems that
can perform any intellectual task a human can do. Current AI systems are considered "narrow
AI" because they excel only at specific tasks. Multi-modal AI, combining vision, language,
and reasoning, is a major area of current research progress.
